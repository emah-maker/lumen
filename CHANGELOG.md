# Changelog

Notable changes to Lumen. Versions follow the tags on GitHub; downloads are on [Releases](https://github.com/emah-maker/lumen/releases).

## Unreleased

- **Auto picks the model by what you are doing, not just how hard it sounds.** Quick chat, page questions, summaries, translations, short emails and picture requests go to the small, fast model. One-step browsing goes to Haiku (or the mid-size model of providers whose small model is weaker at clicking), multi-step browsing, forms, research, comparisons, cover letters and other long-form writing go to the mid-size model, hard debugging and proofs go to the strongest, and background tasks never use the smallest. The reply’s tooltip says why ("Auto: Sonnet 5.5 for a multi-step browsing task"). It also knows the GPT-6 (Luna, Sol, Astra) and Gemini 3.x names, knows the GPT-6 and Grok 4.3+ context windows, and prefers Opus over Fable when both would do. The same rules now apply when you pick Default on Codex, Grok Build or Antigravity (as they already did for Claude Code), and Settings → AI explains it in one line, and `docs/auto-model.md` now has the situation table and the research behind it.

- **Try again on a card that couldn’t update works again, and cards get past a network hiccup on their own.** Clicking Try again right after an automatic retry did nothing (a card was fetched at most every 15 s, even one showing an error); now it always asks again (a double click is one request). And after a network glitch that wasn’t a sleep (an update restart, a Wi-Fi change) Lumen could keep a dead connection or a stale DNS answer, so every retry failed with “Couldn’t connect” until Lumen was restarted. Before a card that failed that way asks again, Lumen now drops those first, as it already did on waking from sleep.
- **The "AI is using this tab" pill shows only on the tab the AI is working in.** While a task ran in one tab, the toolbar pill stayed on every tab you switched to (saying the AI was in another tab). Now it appears only while the tab in front is the AI's tab, follows you as you switch tabs, and goes when the task ends; the sidebar's "Working in" line still says where the AI is.

## 0.5.21 (2026-10-09)

- **Settings looks calmer and reads faster.** Each page opens with a short line saying what it holds, and its title stays at the top while you scroll. A heading that only repeated its first row is said once (On startup, Search engine). Descriptions show two lines, with a small Show more only where the text is really cut off. Rows that held a value and buttons now put them on one line (Downloads location), stacked controls line up under their label, and the folded lists (Spell check languages, Language packs and site lists) are full-width rows with a chevron on the right like the other link rows. Searching highlights the matched words. Buttons, switches and fields get a press state and firmer hover and focus rings, motion is cut when the system asks for reduced motion, the content column is a steady 820 px wide and centered, and the sidebar turns into a row of categories below 800 px (was 720). The Task manager in Advanced no longer keeps a 2-second timer running in other sections: it refreshes only while it is on screen and the tab is visible.
- **More everyday browsing polish.** Ctrl+Plus and Ctrl+Minus (and Ctrl+wheel) now step through Chrome's zoom stops (25, 33, 50, 67, 75, 80, 90, 100, 110, 125, 150, 175, 200, 250, 300, 400, 500%) instead of about 10% at a time, so the zoom pill always shows a familiar number; each site still remembers its level and Ctrl+0 still resets. Reopen Closed Tab (Ctrl+Shift+T, the menus, tab search's Recently closed) brings a tab back pinned if it was, in its group if the group still exists, in the place it had in the strip, and with its back and forward history, not just its address. The downloads panel, the Downloads, History and Bookmarks pages and the error page take their wording from the language files (`locales/en.json`, keys `dlpanel.*`, `dlpage.*`, `historyPage.*`, `bookmarksPage.*`, `errorPage.*`) like the rest of Lumen, so a translation reaches them. The downloads panel updates each download's row in place instead of rebuilding the list on every report, so a click always lands on the row you pressed and the focused button stays focused. Ctrl+F with text selected on the page (or in a text field, never a password field) opens the find bar on that text, selected, as Chrome does. New tests: `test/zoom-steps-units.js`, `test/closed-tabs-units.js`, `test/page-i18n-units.js`, `test/downloads-panel-units.js`, `test/find-prefill-units.js`.
- **Everyday browsing polish.** `intranet:8080` and `nas:5000/admin` open as addresses (they were searched), and `999.1.1.1` is searched instead of opened. F5, Ctrl+R and the tab menu's Reload now reload a page that is still loading (they stopped it; only the toolbar button is Stop). New keys: F6 and Alt+D focus the address bar, F3 / Shift+F3 and Ctrl+G / Ctrl+Shift+G (Cmd+G) step through find matches or open the find bar on the last search, Ctrl+F4 closes the tab. The mouse's back and forward buttons and a keyboard's browser keys (and the two-finger swipe on macOS) now move through history. Right-clicking the address bar, Find or the sidebar's message box now shows Cut / Copy / Paste / Select All, and the address bar adds Paste and Go (or Paste and Search for words). Download progress reaches the panel a few times a second instead of on every report, so its buttons no longer get replaced under the pointer mid-click and big downloads cost less to track. The error page says what happened for more errors (DNS timeout, failed proxy tunnel, malformed address, unknown scheme, blocked by firewall, and more) and the certificate warning explains more certificate errors than before. Reader mode's button tooltip is translated. New tests: `test/core-browsing-units.js`.
- **Less main-process work while pages load and tabs change.** The tab strip is no longer re-sent (nor the sidebar's target, the chat page and the session timer re-run) when a page event changed nothing it shows, such as repeated stop-loading events or an identical favicon. The language probe on each page load now sends a small script instead of the whole 9 KB translator, the translator and reader-mode probes send their source once per page and only the call afterwards, and the ad blocker works out each page's on/off answer once instead of for every request.
- **The animated new-tab effect wakes the page half as often and rests when you are away.** The effect loop used to ask for an animation frame 60 times a second and throw about half of them away; it now waits out the interval with a timer and asks for exactly the frames it draws (30 a second, 20 in Performance mode). After 90 seconds without pointer, key or wheel input it stops (the canvas keeps the last frame) and starts again on the next input. Nothing changes with the effect off (the script is never loaded, as before). New tests: `test/newtab-effects-units.js`.
- **Spotify card search is steadier while you type.** The results no longer go blank and jump between keys (the rows that were on screen stay, dimmed, until the new answer arrives), pictures arriving no longer rebuild the list (so the scroll position, the highlighted row and Enter stay put), the pause before a search is 250 ms, and an older answer can never replace a newer one. Spotify's rate limit (429) is waited out quietly instead of showing a failure; a long one reads as a calm "asked Lumen to slow down" line.
- **Agents that are not the chat you are typing into stay out of your window.** An outside agent over MCP keeps its step lines, "connected" notices and approval cards in its own window; your window only gets a small count badge in the toolbar when one of them waits for your OK (click it to go to that window; it never opens the sidebar or takes focus). Background tasks and routines no longer pop a banner into your window when they finish or need you (the Background tasks button's badge and the system notification when Lumen is not in front remain). The helpers of `delegate` read out of sight: they no longer open research tabs in your tab strip, and the chat that started them shows the delegate step and the final answers instead of a row per helper that expands as it works. And the sidebar chat itself now loads pages in a tab of its own (a new tab behind yours, marked as the AI's, reused for the chat's later steps) instead of navigating the tab you are looking at, unless your message is about the current page ("summarize this", "what is this error", a selection, an attached tab). Opt-in Chrome DevTools automation and macros you run yourself are unchanged. New tests: `test/agents-quiet-units.js`.
- **Spotify card: playlist tabs (Now playing card, large and up).** Click a playlist in the Library tab and its songs open inside the card, with Back, Play and Shuffle; click a song to play the playlist from there. The pin button on a Library row (or Pin in the open playlist) makes it its own tab after the built-in ones (up to four, kept across restarts; Unpin or the Delete key removes it). Uses only Spotify calls that still exist after the February 2026 changes (`/playlists/{id}/items`, `/albums/{id}`, `PUT /me/player/play` with `context_uri` and `offset`). "Play as Lumen" is unchanged for now.
- **Less memory when you are not looking at the new-tab page.** A new-tab page left in a background tab now unloads after 10 minutes (it holds nothing and comes back in a moment); before, it stayed loaded for as long as the tab existed. While a new-tab page is hidden its animated effect asks for no animation frames at all and gives its pixel buffer back after 20 seconds, its TradingView charts (each a separate process of about 130 MB) are parked after 45 seconds and load again the moment the page is shown, and the effect's canvas is capped at about 2.6 million pixels on very large windows. The hidden Spotify and Apple Music player page used to count its own 5-second heartbeat as use, so it never reached its idle unload; now only playing music or a card reading it counts, and it unloads after 5 idle minutes (was 15) and starts again on demand. What you see while the page is in front is unchanged.
- **An outside agent (Claude Code over MCP) stays off your tabs, focus and clipboard.** Its tools already ran in a Lumen window of its own; this closes the gaps found in an audit. If you close that window while a call is running, the call now stops instead of carrying on in whichever of your windows is current. Naming one of your tabs in `switch_tab`, `close_tab`, `group_tabs`, `ungroup_tabs` or `read_tabs` is refused with a message saying the tab is yours and the agent works only in its own window's tabs (the real tab is never switched, grouped, closed or read). The agent's `clipboard` tool uses a private buffer for its session: it no longer overwrites what you copied, and it never reads it. A page in the agent's window can no longer start a download into your Downloads folder (which could also open a save dialog or an approval card in your window), and Lumen never moves the keyboard focus inside an agent window you are not in. New unit tests cover each rule (`test/agent-hands-off-units.js`).
- **Waking a sleeping tab no longer shows a screenshot first.** The picture of the old page that stood in while a tab reloaded is gone (it looked wrong and cost CPU and disk to take); the tab shows the normal page load on the theme's own background, so there is no white flash in dark mode. Lumen no longer takes these pictures, deletes the old `tab-snapshots` folder once on update, and the "Show a preview while a sleeping tab wakes" setting is gone. To make waking faster instead: a sleeping tab starts loading when the pointer rests on it for 100 ms (was 150), when you press on it, and when a keyboard switch is about to reach it. Idle tabs are now frozen in memory for 10 minutes before they unload (a frozen tab comes back instantly; change it in Settings > Tabs > Memory), the 3 most recently used background tabs are never put to sleep by the idle timer (new "Always keep the most recent tabs awake" setting; low memory and the cap still can), and pinned tabs never sleep by default. Tabs playing audio or holding typed text were already kept awake and still are.
- **New features that ship off are offered after an update.** Right after the What's new notes, Lumen asks once about each new opt-in feature ("Let the AI use files on this computer" is the first): Turn on applies it at once, Not now leaves it off, and it's never asked again; the switch stays in Settings. A fresh install is never asked (features/feature-offers.js).
- **Pinch to zoom.** A trackpad (or touch screen) pinch now magnifies the page in place, as in Chrome, in normal and private windows; the page keeps its layout, and Ctrl/Cmd + and − still change page zoom. Settings > Appearance > "Pinch to zoom" (on by default).
- **The AI can use files on your computer, drag, and use the clipboard.** New setting Settings > AI > "Let the AI use files on this computer" (off by default). With it on, `upload_file` takes `paths` (for example `~/Desktop/screenshot.png`), so "upload the screenshot on my desktop to this form" works without picking the file yourself, and the new `list_files` tool finds files (Desktop by default, newest first, `*.png`-style filters); the clipboard tool can read the clipboard. Lumen's own profile, `~/.ssh`, `~/.aws`, `~/.gnupg`, keychains and `.env`/key files are always refused, also through links. The first upload to a site in a chat still names the files on a card unless you chose Auto-allow or Bypass. New `drag` tool for every engine and MCP client: drags sliders, sortable lists and HTML5 drop zones (real mouse input through DevTools, works in background tabs). `clipboard` with `action: "write"` needs no setting.
- **The AI's drawings now stay on the right spot when the window changes, and it has more tools to draw with.** Resizing the window, opening or closing the sidebar, zooming, moving to a screen with another pixel ratio, or a page reflowing used to leave marks where they were while the content moved. Each mark is now pinned to the page content under it (the element, and where the mark sits inside it) and is recomputed when the window, the page's size, the zoom or the pixel ratio changes, falling back to the page position if that element is removed. The overlay is re-fitted to the window at once, so it is never stretched. New mark types for the annotate tool: strike (strikethrough), bracket (a curly brace), check and cross, redact (a box that hides something), callout (a note with a leader line to its target) and path (a freehand stroke), plus options for line width, opacity and straight or curved arrows.
- **Cards that load from the internet no longer give up after one bad minute.** A calendar (or any other card) that hit a network hiccup (offline, a DNS lookup failing, a connection reset, a timeout, a 5xx from the server) now asks again by itself after 2 seconds, 10 seconds and 30 seconds, and then goes back to its normal schedule. It also asks again when the computer wakes up or is unlocked, when the browser reports it is back online, and when you come back to the new-tab page. While it is down, a card that has shown something keeps showing it, with "Updated 40 min ago" and a short "Couldn't refresh; showing earlier events" note instead of an error screen. The "Couldn't connect. Check your internet connection." message is now only used for connection trouble: a timeout says the server took too long, and a refused certificate says it couldn't connect securely. The calendar's wait for a feed went from 8 to 12 seconds (a large feed, about 190 KB, was never near any size limit; the cap is 5 MB).
- **The Todoist card scrolls and is easier to add tasks to.** The list now scrolls inside the card (wheel, trackpad, keyboard, with the same thin scrollbar as the other cards) instead of cutting rows off, and it shows more than the card's "tasks shown" setting when there is room, with "Show N more" and "Show fewer" for the rest. A new "+ Add task" row (or an always-open box at the top or bottom; Settings > Add-task box is now Button, Top or Bottom, and defaults to Top) adds a task as you type it, Todoist-style ("Pay rent tomorrow 5pm"): Enter adds and keeps the box ready for the next one, Esc clears it, the new task appears at once and is taken away again with a note if Todoist refuses it. On a Today card a new task with no day gets today so it doesn't vanish; on a project card it goes into that project. A small arrow on each task moves it to Today, Tomorrow, next week or no date (not for repeating tasks), and a Today / Upcoming / Inbox switch at the top of the card changes what it shows.
- **Subscripts and superscripts in AI replies now render.** A reply with `σ<sub>x</sub>` used to show the raw tags; now `<sub>` and `<sup>` (and `H~2~O`, `x^2^`) draw as real subscripts and superscripts in the sidebar chat, the chat page and /btw. Only those four bare tags are allowed (no attributes, properly paired); anything else stays escaped, and code is untouched. Copying a reply, exporting a chat and notifications give plain text such as `σ_x` instead of tags.

## 0.5.20 (2026-10-08)

- **The Spotify card's Library and Up next tabs work again in "Play as Lumen".** Spotify changed its web player: the library list no longer has links and the queue is now a side panel instead of a page, so Lumen found nothing and the tabs stayed on "Loading…". Lumen now reads the library from the page's new rows (Liked Songs is not listed) and opens the Queue panel to read what is next, and "play from the queue" presses that row's own play button. If a tab still can't be read it now says "Couldn't read your library." with a Try again button instead of loading forever. Shuffle stays hidden when the page shows no shuffle button.
- **The Spotify card in Web API mode follows Spotify's February 2026 changes.** The heart and saving a song use the new library endpoints, and an artist playing has no track list (Spotify removed it), which the Album tab now says.
- **The Spotify card in Web API mode gets more of what "Play as Lumen" has.** Your Library tab lists your Liked Songs, and search has "More songs" (up to 30, ten per request, the most Spotify allows). Lyrics stay out of this mode: Spotify's Web API has no lyrics.
- **A cleaner volume control on the music cards.** A thin rounded track filled in the accent colour, a small round thumb, a speaker button that mutes and unmutes (and shows the level), and a keyboard focus ring; it reads on the plain card, frosted glass and wallpapers, light or dark.
- **Sonnet 5.5 replaces Sonnet 5 in the model picker, and Haiku 5.5 is priced right.** The picker offers Sonnet 5.5 (same price as Sonnet 5); chats saved on Sonnet 5 run on Sonnet 5.5 and older replies keep their "Sonnet 5" label. OpenRouter's default Claude is Sonnet 5.5. The usage estimate for Haiku 5.5 was ten times too high ($1 / $5 per million tokens instead of $0.10 / $0.50).

- **An outside agent (Claude Code over MCP) no longer fills your window with its steps.** Lines like "Claude Code: Running a script on the page" (one per call) were added to the sidebar chat of the window you were using, along with a "connected" notice and the "driven by" pill, even though the agent works in a window of its own. Its steps now go only to that window, where the pill shows the latest one; your window gets nothing but an approval card when one is needed (which still opens nothing). Where step lines still appear, the same step repeated in a row is one line with a count.

- **The AI can open and read local PDFs.** Asking it to open a PDF on your computer used to end in "my browser tools only accept http and https pages". `navigate` and `open_tab` now take a Windows path (spaces and OneDrive folders are fine, quotes are ignored) or a `file:///` address ending in `.pdf`, and show it in Lumen's PDF viewer like File > Open; `read_pdf` and `read_page` then work on it. Only existing `.pdf` files are allowed: folders, other file types, network paths (`\\server\share`) and device paths are refused, symlinks and `..` are resolved first, and every other `file://` address stays blocked. It asks once per file per chat ("Allow the AI to open and read <name>?", shown with the file name only); that answer also covers `read_pdf`. Web pages cannot trigger it.

- **Auto sends more work to Haiku 5.5.** Haiku is far more capable now, so Auto (the picker's Auto, each provider's Auto and Claude Code's "Pick the Claude Code model for me") gives the fast tier everyday chat, questions about the page, summaries, email drafts and plain browsing, not just greetings and lookups; messages about code or config (fix, bug, function, test, SQL, API, a code fence) stay on Sonnet, and Opus is kept for clearly hard multi-step briefs (the "heavy" score moved from 5 to 6). A long conversation or attachment is only lifted off the fast model past 120,000 characters (it was 60,000; attachments 80,000, was 40,000). Haiku 5.5 (`claude-haiku-5-5`) replaces Haiku 4.5 in the model list, in helpers, side questions, tab-group suggestions and the cost estimate; a chat saved on Haiku 4.5 now runs as Haiku 5.5. A model you pick yourself is always used.

- **The AI's drawings land where it points and stay until you press Esc.** Marks placed by screenshot position are now converted with the screenshot's own scale (page width over image width, per axis) and the scroll position it was taken at, so a changed page zoom, a different display scaling (125%, 150%, 200%) or a page scrolled since the screenshot no longer shifts them. A drawing also no longer disappears when the page changes its address without reloading (single-page sites) or when the page's own script removes the overlay: only Esc, "Clear drawings" or a real navigation ends it.

## 0.5.19 (2026-10-08)

- **Research: a red "Retracted" label now needs Crossref to confirm it.** OpenAlex's retraction flag has false positives (it marked the Lancet 2020 dementia commission report, which is not retracted), so OpenAlex or PubMed alone now shows an amber "Retraction flag (unconfirmed)" chip (tooltip: check the publisher's page); "Retracted (Crossref)" in red appears only when Crossref's Retraction Watch data (or a "RETRACTED:" title) says so. Crossref expressions of concern and corrections get their own softer chips, and the AI is told to confirm on the publisher's page and not to call an unconfirmed flag a retraction.


- **New-tab pages and the music cards stay quiet when nothing is moving.** The new-tab page used to run a handful of one-second timers all the time (clocks, countdowns, stacks, the music progress bar), also in the spare page and in the background. They now exist only while the page is on screen and something needs them (a clock showing seconds, a running timer, a playing song, a stack that turns by itself), line up with the second, and are gone otherwise: a new-tab page with nothing time-based is idle. The Spotify and Apple Music Web player card no longer asks the page where it is five times a second; the page tells Lumen when the card moves or resizes, and the Spotify page reader and the YouTube ad check react to page changes instead of polling.
- **Spotify search and the card's buttons are faster in "Play as Lumen".** Search shows its first rows as soon as Spotify's page draws them (median 1.0 s instead of 1.7 s on open.spotify.com; the whole list 1.5 s instead of 2.3 s) and opens one page route instead of two ("More songs" loads the longer list on request); with Spotify connected to the Web API a search is one request (about 150 ms) whatever plays the music. Search as you type waits 200 ms after the last key, starts at two letters (Enter searches at once), remembers your last 50 searches for ten minutes, shows outlines while it loads, and loads pictures only for the rows on screen. Searching has its own request budget, so polling never makes it wait, and the hidden player starts when you click into the search box. Pressing play, shuffle, repeat or the heart changes the card in the same moment (and rolls back if the player did not do it), a volume drag is heard as you drag, and the card hears what the page did after 80 ms instead of 400 ms.
- **Research framework: `/research`, scholarly source search, a Research board and citations.** `/research <question>` (options `--quick`, `--deep`, `--academic`, `--since 2020` or `--years 2015-2024`) plans 3 to 6 sub-questions as a checklist (it asks one question first only when the request is truly ambiguous), runs them side by side in helpers, reads the sources that matter, and answers with a numbered citation after every claim, `[1]`, linking to the exact passage (a text-fragment link `#:~:text=` for web pages, `#page=N` for PDFs), then a Sources list and a "gaps, conflicting evidence, what to verify" section. The new `find_sources` tool searches OpenAlex, Crossref, Semantic Scholar, arXiv and PubMed at once (free, no key, nothing about you is sent), merges and dedupes by DOI or title, and returns authors, year, venue, DOI, an open-access PDF link, citation counts, an abstract snippet and the retraction flag; `related` + a DOI follows who cites a paper or what it cites. Every source gets small chips: type (peer-reviewed journal, preprint, government, news, blog, forum, Wikipedia...), year (and "dated"), citation count, retracted (and which database says so), `.gov` / `.edu` / known publisher, open access, and whether the quote was found in the text the AI actually read. A new Research board in the sidebar keeps every source the AI or you add, per chat and saved with it: star, note, pinned quotes, open in a tab, remove, and "Add this page" for the tab in front (title, authors, date, journal, DOI and PDF link from Google Scholar `citation_*` tags, Dublin Core or JSON-LD). Copy one citation or the whole bibliography as APA 7, MLA 9, Chicago (author-date), IEEE, BibTeX or RIS, or ask the AI to "cite this in MLA". Sources stay untrusted page content, a site with AI turned off is hidden from the AI, and in a chat that has read a page the search asks first like `web_search` does. Titles and journal names are used as the databases give them, so check capitalisation and abbreviations against your style guide.

- "Preload tabs after startup" now defaults to 1 tab instead of 2, which saves about 250 MB of memory. You can raise it in Settings > Tabs > Memory.

- **Tabs that were asleep or restored come back faster.** Switching to an unloaded or not-yet-opened tab now shows a small picture of the page as you last saw it at once (about 20 ms, against about 350 ms of blank), until the real page has painted. Resting the pointer on a sleeping tab for 150 ms, or pressing it, starts loading it before the click completes (and Ctrl+Tab wakes the tab after the next one); a page that was loaded by the time you click is on screen instantly. After start-up, once the first page has loaded and the computer is idle, Lumen also loads the tabs next to it one at a time (Settings > Tabs > Memory > "Preload tabs after startup", 0/1/2/3/5, default 2, about 200 to 250 MB each; stops under memory pressure, in Performance mode, and on battery after one). Pictures are JPEGs in the profile's `tab-snapshots` folder (50 MB cap, oldest out; about 35 KB each), never taken in private windows, of sign-in/payment/account pages, pages with password or card fields, or addresses with tokens, and wiped with history and "cached images and files" (Settings > Tabs > Memory can turn them off). New, off by default: "Keep recently idle tabs frozen first" (10/30/60 minutes) freezes an idle tab before unloading it, so a return within that time is instant.
- **Lumen does less work while it sits idle.** A background tab whose title ticks no longer makes Lumen rewrite your settings file every few seconds (and an unchanged session or settings file is not rewritten at all: about 3.5 writes a minute to none in a measured idle run), the sidebar's speculative Claude Code process is released after 3 minutes instead of 10 (as Codex and Grok already were), and the always-on housekeeping timers (chat run slots, tab sleeping, the spare new-tab page, AI status) now share one scheduler and one memory reading that run less often while every window is minimised or the screen is locked. `node scripts/measure-idle.js` counts the wakeups and writes.
- **Macros: save a sequence of steps and run it again.** Settings > Macros makes a macro by recording what you do in a page (clicks, typing, menu choices, keys, tab switches; password and card fields are never read, a Pause step waits for you instead), by describing it in a sentence for the AI to draft, or by adding steps by hand: open a page, click, type, choose an option, press a key, wait (seconds, an element, the load, some text), scroll, switch or close a tab, run a Lumen command (reader mode, mute, pin, group, sidebar prompt, bookmark, zoom and more), ask the AI, or pause for you. Elements are found again by test id, id, role and name, text, then a CSS selector, so a page that changes a little still works. `{{query}}` asks for a value each time; `{{clipboard}}`, `{{selection}}`, `{{url}}`, `{{title}}`, `{{date}}` and `{{time}}` are filled in. Give a macro a keyboard shortcut (checked against Lumen's own and your other macros) or limit it to one site. Run one from ⋯ > Macros, its shortcut, `/macro name` in the sidebar, or Run in Settings; a toast shows step N/M with Stop, and a failed step says which step and why, with Edit macro. The AI can run your macros with the new `run_macro` tool, through the same approval cards as its own actions plus one for any step that submits, buys or sends; it never runs a macro that uses your clipboard or asks the AI. Export and import as JSON (reviewed first). Steps are the AI's own click, type, navigate and wait tools, with no second automation engine. See [docs/macros.md](docs/macros.md).
- **The Spotify and Apple Music cards do more as you make them bigger.** Small: art, title, play / pause and next. Medium: also previous, a seek bar with times, the heart (save / love), shuffle, repeat and volume. Large: tabs inside the card for Search (results you can play or add to the queue), Up next (the queue: play from it), Library (recent plays and playlists) and Devices (Spotify API mode: pick where it plays; Play as Lumen: this browser). Extra large: the album or playlist playing as a track list (play from here) and lyrics where the service has them (Apple Music for subscribers, Spotify through Play as Lumen). The card is updated in place, so a typed search, an open tab and a slider you are dragging stay put; Space plays and pauses a focused card, the arrow keys seek and change the volume. A control an engine cannot do is not shown. Spotify's API mode now also asks for the playlist, recent plays and saved-songs permissions; a card connected before this keeps working and offers to reconnect when you use the heart or your library.
- **The AI can draw on the page to explain it.** A new `annotate` tool circles or boxes an element, points arrows, numbers steps (the numbers match the written steps), highlights or underlines text, adds short callout labels and dims everything but one spotlighted area, like a teacher marking a screenshot. Marks are pinned to an element id from `read_page` / `find`, or to `text:…` on the page, so they follow it on scroll, resize and zoom; marks placed from a screenshot are anchored to the page. They are drawn in a closed, click-through overlay the page cannot read (hand-drawn, high-contrast with a halo, animated unless Reduce motion is on), with a Clear drawings pill and Esc to remove them, and they go on navigation. In Lumen's PDF viewer they stay on their PDF page through scroll and zoom. The sidebar shows `Drew N marks` with Show again and Clear. Where an overlay can't go (Chrome's own PDF viewer, or a page that refuses it) the marks are drawn on a screenshot shown in the chat. It changes nothing on the page, so it asks no approval, is refused on sites with AI turned off, and Grok Build, Claude Code and other MCP clients can use it too.
