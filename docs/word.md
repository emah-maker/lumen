# The AI working in Microsoft Word

Lumen's AI can work with Word documents directly: read them with their headings, lists, tables, comments and tracked changes, add and change text, apply styles, insert tables and pictures, add comments, turn tracked changes on, and save. This is more reliable than clicking around Word (see [The AI using other apps](desktop-apps.md)), because the AI talks to the document itself rather than to a picture of it.

It is on by default (Settings → AI → **Let the AI use Microsoft Word**) and costs nothing until the AI first works with a document: nothing is started, and the tool is offered only when the conversation is about a Word document: you name one ("in Word", `.docx`), say "my essay / report / resume..." or "this doc", ask for a report, essay, letter, memo or formatted document to be written ("write a report on...", "draft a cover letter"), attach a .docx/.doc/.rtf/.odt, have one open in a Lumen tab, or a file tool (list_files, a download) shows one. A Google Doc in front is the `document` tool's, never Word's. The tool says to use it for all of these, to make a new document for something to be written instead of pasting it into the chat as Markdown, and never to write scripts for it. In the sidebar's API chats the message also carries a one-line reminder until the chat first uses the tool. The routing is `src/ai/app-intent.js` (`test/units/app-intent-units.js`).

## What it can do

One tool, `word`, with these actions:

| Action | What it does |
|---|---|
| `list` | The documents open in Word. |
| `open` / `new` | Open a `.docx`, `.doc`, `.rtf`, `.odt` or `.txt` file (by full path) in Word, or start a new document (optionally saved straight to a new `.docx`). |
| `read` | The document's text with its structure (`#` headings, `-` lists, `\| tables \|`), comments and a tracked-changes summary, in chunks (`offset` pages through a long one). |
| `insert` | New paragraphs at the end, the start, after a heading or paragraph, or at the end of a heading's section, in a style (Normal, Heading 1 to 6, Title, List Bullet, List Number, Quote, or one the document has by its name), optionally bold or italic. |
| `insert` with `find` | Inside a paragraph, right after the first place `find` matches: `text` (one line, in the look of the text before it); `note: footnote` or `endnote` with `text` (the note's mark goes there, its text into the notes); or `ref` (`"Figure 2"`, `"Table 1"`, or a bookmark's name: a cross-reference Word keeps up to date, with `text` before it if given). |
| `replace` | Find and replace text (exact case, every match, with the count). `replace` is required (`""` deletes the found text); a line break in `find` matches across paragraphs, and one in `replace` starts a new paragraph. An edit given an argument it does not take (such as `new_text`) is refused and changes nothing. `whole_word` matches whole words only, and the new text can be given a look: `bold`, `italic`, `underline`, `color`, `highlight`, `strike`, `font`, `size`. |
| `style` | Formatting on the exact text found (every match): a paragraph style (built in or the document's own, by name) and character formatting: `bold`, `italic`, `underline` (`true` or single, double, thick, dotted, dash, wave), `color` (a name or `#RRGGBB`), `highlight` (Word's highlight colors), `strike`, `font`, `size`. `link` (`https://...`, `mailto:`, or `#bookmark`) makes every match a hyperlink, and `bookmark` (a name) marks the first match, for `ref` and `#` links. |
| `format` | The layout in one call: `font`, `size`, `line_spacing` (1, 1.15, 1.5, 2 as a multiple; 6 or more is an exact height in points), `space_before` / `space_after` (points), for the whole document, one `style`, or the paragraphs holding some text (`find`); `margins` (`normal`, `narrow`, `moderate`, `wide`, or inches: `"1"`, `"1 1.25"`, `"1 1 1 1"`) and `orientation`, for every section (or `section` n); `page_break_before` (paragraph texts); `headings` (`"# Abstract"`, `"## Methods"`: Heading 1 to 3); `toc: true`, a real table of contents. |
| `format`: paragraphs, styles, lists | Where the change goes: `style` (that style everywhere), `find` (the paragraphs holding that text), `paragraphs` (a list of paragraph texts) or nothing (the whole document). Besides fonts and spacing: `bold`, `italic`, `underline`, `color`, `align` (left, center, right, justify), `indent` (inches), `first_line` (inches; negative is a hanging indent), `keep_with_next`. `style` with `based_on` makes a new paragraph style when the document has none of that name (or changes what an existing one is based on). `list`: `bullet`, `number` or `none` on the paragraphs, at `level` 1 to 9; each numbered list starts at 1. `headings` takes `#` to `######` (Heading 1 to 6). |
| `format`: pages | `header` and `footer` (`"text"`, or `"left | center | right"`, with `{page}`, `{pages}` and `{date}` as Word fields: `footer: "Page {page} of {pages}"`), `page_numbers` (`true`, or where: `bottom center`, `bottom right`, `top right`...), `different_first_page` (a title page without them), `page_number_start`, `columns` (1 to 4); `section` for one section only. `section_break_before` (paragraph texts) with `section_break` (`next_page`, `continuous`, `even_page`, `odd_page`), and `column_break_before`. Breaks are made first, so a `section` number in the same call counts the sections they make. `properties` (`{ title, author, subject, keywords }`) sets the document's own properties (File > Info). |
| `table` | Insert a table from `rows`, with its look: `header` (the first row repeats on each page and is bold; on by default for more than one row), `widths` (inches per column), `borders` (`all`, `outside`, `none`), `shading` (the header row's fill color), `align` (`left`, `center`, `right`). Or change a table that is there, picked by `index` (1 is the first table, in reading order) or `find` (text in one of its cells): `cells` (`[[row, column, "text"], ...]`, 1-based, counted after this call's new rows and column), `add_rows` (after row n: `0` is the top, `"end"` the bottom) with `rows`, `add_column` (after column n, or `"end"`), and the same look fields. `delete_rows` / `delete_columns` (`3`, or a range `[2, 4]`, as the table is now, in a call of their own) take rows or columns out; the content guard's remove card shows what goes first, every time, and the last row or column is never deleted (the AI is told to remove the whole table instead). A table with merged cells keeps its columns when columns are added or widths set. |
| `image` | Insert a picture (`.png`, `.jpg`, `.gif`, `.bmp`) from a file or an `https` address: `width` / `height` (inches; one of them keeps the shape; otherwise its own size, no wider than the text), `wrap` (`inline`, `square`, `tight`, `top_bottom`, `behind`, `front`), `align` (`left`, `center` (the default), `right`), `caption` ("Figure N: ..." under it, numbered by Word), `alt` (text for screen readers). A new `table` takes `caption` too ("Table N: ..." above it). Read shows a picture as `[picture]`. |
| `comment` / `track` | Add a comment on some text. `track` with `on` turns tracked changes on or off, so the AI's edits show as revisions you can accept or reject; with `accept: true` or `reject: true` it accepts or rejects the tracked changes there are: all of them, one author's (`author: "AI (Lumen)"` undoes the AI's own edits), or those in the paragraphs holding `find`. |
| `save` / `save_as` | Save the document, or save a copy as `.docx` or `.pdf`. |
| `show` | Bring Word to the front at the place the AI last changed. |
| `help` | Every action's fields, or those for a topic (`topic`: "lists", "style", "format pages"). The tool's own description is kept short (it is sent with every request in a chat about Word), so it names the common fields and points here for the rest. Reads nothing and needs no Word. |

## How it works

**On Windows with Word installed**, Lumen drives Word's own automation (COM) through one hidden PowerShell helper. It starts the first time the AI uses the tool and ends after a quiet spell. Every value (document text, find text, file names) travels to the helper as data in a JSON line; nothing the AI or a document says is ever put into a script or a command line. If Word is already running, the AI works in that Word; otherwise Lumen starts Word. A document opened from a OneDrive folder is listed by Word under its web address; the AI can still name it by its local path. If Word is closed or stops answering mid-task, the helper lets it go and the AI is told to list and reopen the document. Lumen never closes your documents, and it quits a Word only if Lumen started it and no document is open in it. Macros never run (documents are opened with macros disabled).

**Without Word** (not installed, or not Windows), a `.docx` file can still be read, and given new paragraphs, headings and tables by writing into the file itself. Those edits are written as tracked changes by "AI (Lumen)", the file is replaced in one step (a failure leaves the original untouched), and a card shows the file's path first. `format` works on the file too: fonts and spacing go into `word/styles.xml` (the document defaults and Normal, or the one style; a whole-document font also replaces the font every style names and drops the runs' own fonts, as "select all" would), margins and orientation into each section's `w:sectPr` (`w:pgMar`, `w:pgSz`), a page break is a paragraph holding `<w:br w:type="page"/>` (none is added where a page already starts), and the table of contents is a `TOC \o "1-3" \h \z \u` field under the "Table of Contents" title (the typed lines under it are taken out, as tracked deletions) with `<w:updateFields w:val="true"/>` in `word/settings.xml` (made if missing), so Word fills it in when the document opens. New text, page breaks and removed lines are tracked changes; style and page changes are not. In Word, `format` does the same through Word's own styles, page setup, `InsertBreak` and `TablesOfContents.Add`, and the table of contents is filled in at once. The `style` action works on the file too: the found text is cut out of its run into one of its own (the runs around it keep their properties), in body text and tables. A style named in `insert`, `style` or `format` is looked up by its name in `word/styles.xml`; a built-in one the file lacks is added, and `format` with `based_on` makes a new one (`w:customStyle`). Lists are real Word lists: one nine-level definition per kind in `word/numbering.xml` (made if missing, with its relationship and content type; bullets • ◦ ▪, numbers 1. a. i.) and a `w:num` of its own for each new list, so a numbered list starts at 1; each list item has its `w:numPr` level. In Word, the same calls use Word's own styles (`Styles.Add` with a base style), its default bullet and number list templates (`ApplyListTemplateWithLevel`), and the range's highlight. Tables change in the file too: a new table's look goes into its `w:tblPr` (`w:jc`, `w:tblBorders`, a fixed `w:tblLayout` with widths), `w:tblGrid`, each cell's `w:tcW` and `w:shd`, and the header row's `w:tblHeader`; a changed cell keeps its first paragraph's and run's look, its old text a tracked deletion and the new text a tracked insertion in the same paragraph; a new row copies the cell widths of a data row and is a tracked row (`w:trPr`/`w:ins`); a new column goes into every row and the grid. Deleting rows or columns in the file takes them out for good (not a tracked change; the card asked first): a deleted column leaves `w:tblGrid`, a merged cell across it gets a smaller `w:gridSpan` and width, and a vertical merge whose first row went starts in the next row (`w:vMerge w:val="restart"`). In Word, rows and columns go with `Rows.Item(n).Delete()` / `Columns.Item(n).Delete()` (tracked when tracking is on), after a preview for the card. In Word, the same calls use `Rows.Add`, `Columns.Add`, `HeadingFormat`, `Borders`, `Shading` and column widths. Pictures go into the file too: the bytes into `word/media` (with an image relationship and a content type for the extension), and a drawing (`wp:inline`, or `wp:anchor` with `wp:wrapSquare`, `wp:wrapTight`, `wp:wrapTopAndBottom` or `wp:wrapNone`) into a paragraph of its own, as a tracked insertion. Captions are paragraphs in the Caption style whose number is a `SEQ Figure` / `SEQ Table` field, as Word's Insert Caption makes them; the numbers already in the document are put in order again. In Word, pictures use `InlineShapes.AddPicture` (and `ConvertToShape` with its `WrapFormat` for a wrapped one) and captions Word's own `InsertCaption`. A picture is checked by its bytes, not its name. One from the web is fetched only after the card: with no cookies, only from a public address (not this computer or its network, also after a redirect), at most 20 MB; for Word it goes through a temp file that is removed after. Headers and footers are parts of their own (`word/headerN.xml`, `word/footerN.xml`, with their relationships and content types) holding one paragraph in the Header or Footer style, on center and right tab stops at the text's width; the sections they are for point at them from the start of their `w:sectPr` (`w:headerReference` / `w:footerReference`, `default`, and `first` with `w:titlePg` for a different first page). Page numbers, page counts and dates are `PAGE`, `NUMPAGES` and `DATE` fields; Word shows their real values when it updates them (on opening and printing). A section break copies the section's `w:sectPr` into the paragraph before the break, and the next section's `w:type` becomes the break's kind; a column break is a `<w:br w:type="column"/>` run at the start of the paragraph; columns are `w:cols`, the first page number `w:pgNumType`. In Word: the sections' `Headers` / `Footers` with `Fields.Add`, `DifferentFirstPageHeaderFooter`, `PageNumbers`, `TextColumns.SetCount` and `InsertBreak`. Notes and references work in the file too: a footnote or endnote is a `w:footnoteReference` / `w:endnoteReference` run (FootnoteReference style) at the spot, and the note itself goes into `word/footnotes.xml` / `word/endnotes.xml` (made with the separator notes Word needs, with their relationships and content types) in the FootnoteText style. A cross-reference to a caption puts a `_Ref` bookmark around the caption's label and number (as Word's Insert Cross-reference does, and reuses one that is there) and a `REF name \h` field at the spot; one to a bookmark is a `REF` to it. A link is a `w:hyperlink` (an external relationship, or `w:anchor` for a bookmark) around the runs of the match, in the Hyperlink style; a bookmark is `w:bookmarkStart` / `w:bookmarkEnd`. The runs are split at the match's edges; text that runs through a field, a picture, or partly into a tracked change or a link is left for Word. Only `https`, `http` and `mailto` links are made. In Word: `Footnotes.Add`, `Endnotes.Add`, `InsertCrossReference`, `Hyperlinks.Add` and `Bookmarks.Add`. The rest works in the file too: `replace` (inside a paragraph) marks each old text deleted and puts the new one beside it, in the old text's look plus any asked for; `track` `accept` / `reject` does what Word's Accept and Reject do (an insertion kept or taken out, a deletion taken out or put back, a formatting change kept or undone from its `w:rPrChange` / `w:pPrChange` record, an inserted or deleted paragraph mark or row), and `on` sets `w:trackRevisions` in `word/settings.xml`; a comment marks the text (`w:commentRangeStart` / `End` and a reference) and goes into `word/comments.xml` by "AI (Lumen)"; properties go into `docProps/core.xml` (made with its relationship and content type); `{date}` in text put into a paragraph is a `DATE` field. In Word: `Revisions` `Accept` / `Reject`, `Find` with `MatchWholeWord`, `BuiltInDocumentProperties`. A `replace` whose `find` spans paragraphs, and saving as PDF, need Word. `.doc`, `.rtf` and `.odt` files need Word.

**Choosing where it works.** Settings → AI → **Work on files in their apps** (`aiFilesInApps`) picks the route for a file that is not already open in Word. **Automatic** (the default) is what is described above: a closed `.docx` is read from the file, and edits open it in Word when Word is installed. **In the app** always opens the document in Word and reads and edits it there, so you can watch, and offers the tool for any Word document in the chat (this setting does not turn the Word switch on). **From the file** reads and writes the `.docx` file itself and does not start Word, including for a new `.docx`; a kind the file route cannot change (`.doc`, `.rtf`) still opens in Word. A document Word already has open is always read and edited in Word, so unsaved changes are never missed.

**In Claude Code, Codex, Grok Build and Antigravity.** The word tool is not in their shared tool list (that list is size-limited); in a chat about a Word document it is added to that chat's own session, together with one line telling the assistant to use the word tool for it (a new document for a report, essay or letter, not Markdown in the chat), to edit in place, and never to write scripts or new files to do it unless you ask. A chat that is not about a Word document sees neither. A chat's tool list is read when its assistant process starts, so a document first mentioned in a later message may need a new chat before the tool appears.

**On macOS** the Word actions are not available yet ("Windows only for now"); the `.docx` file route above works.

## What it asks

- **Editing a document** asks once per document for the chat (**Allow**), with the document and the text shown.
- **After the AI has read something from elsewhere** in the chat (a web page, an email, a connector, another document), every change asks again, with a warning: text it read could have been written to make it do that. Reading a file by path asks too.
- **Always allow changes to this document** (on the edit cards of a saved document): the AI changes that document without a card in every chat, even after it read something from elsewhere; a step in the chat says "Allowed automatically". It is kept by the document's full path (`aiWordAlways`, `src/word/always.js`), only from a real click, and removed in Settings → AI → Privacy → Word documents the AI can read or change. A document that isn't saved yet never gets the button.
- **Always allow reading this document** (on the card that asks to read a saved document after the AI read something from elsewhere): the AI reads that document without a card in every chat; changes still ask. The same list shows it as "Reading" (a document whose changes are always allowed shows "Reading and changes", and is read without a card too).
- **Saving over a file, saving to a new path, creating a file** always asks, with the path shown, every time.
- **Bypass permissions** answers all of these cards for you (reading, changes and saving), like every other card; a step in the chat says what was allowed. Auto-allow does not answer them.
- **Hands-off mode** refuses everything that opens, changes or saves; listing and reading still work.
- Text from a document is data, never instructions: it reaches the AI inside a wrapper that marks it as untrusted.
- Nothing is deleted but what a change is about: there is no action that deletes a file or closes a document. Taking rows or columns out of a table (`delete_rows`, `delete_columns`) shows the content guard's remove card with what goes, every time.
- Document text goes only to the AI provider you chose, as part of the chat, like a page does. Plan mode allows `list` and `read` only. Outside agents connected over MCP and background tasks never get the tool.

## The card

While the AI works in Word, the chat's one live activity card is titled **Word** and shows:

- the document's name as a chip (click it, the card's picture, or its header to bring Word to the front at the place that was changed, or, if nothing was changed yet, the place the AI last read; the picture is a button, so Enter or Space on it does the same) and, in the status line, what is being done ("Writing in Report.docx: adding a table of 3 rows after “Results”");
- a live picture of Word's window, at the same pace and under the same rules as the card for other apps (about 8 a second while it changes, slower while still, nothing while the card is off screen, a call is running, or Word is minimized or hidden; the picture is held in memory only to be shown);
- when Word's window cannot be shown (hidden, minimized, or the file route), a small page preview instead: the paragraphs around the last change, with the changed ones marked (each new change glows in for a moment; not with reduced motion), updated after every change, and the lines being read while the AI reads;
- from the first step on, never just the document's name: until the first picture or change arrives, the page preview shows the document's own paragraphs where the AI is. A card that started on a web page and moved to Word gets Word's live picture too;
- the subtitle says only Working or Done, and the button says **Show in Word**;
- in the status line, what is being done without the document's name again (the chip shows it; "Reading the document", "Saving the document"): "Replacing 2 bullets under “Experience”", "Adding a table of 3 rows after “Results”"; the step row keeps the full label;
- at the end, the last picture and "Done" with the answer.

`src/word/spec.js` (arguments, labels, the untrusted wrapper), `src/word/index.js` (the controller: approvals, taint, routes), `src/word/com.js` and `src/word/host.ps1` (the helper), `src/word/docx.js` (the `.docx` reader and writer), `src/word/card.js` (the card's events and live picture).
