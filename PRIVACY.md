# Lumen privacy policy

Lumen has no account, no servers of its own, no telemetry, no analytics and no crash reporting. The people who make Lumen never receive your browsing, your chats or your keys.

## What stays on your computer

- **Browsing data:** history, bookmarks, open tabs, downloads, cookies and site data are stored in Lumen's profile folder on your computer.
- **Pinned and recent files:** the Files card on the new-tab page and the `Ctrl+Shift+F` picker keep a list of the files and folders you pinned and the local files you opened (at most 500): only each one's path, name, size, modified time and when you opened it, never what is inside, encrypted the same way as the chats. Settings → Privacy and security → Files turns off **Remember files I open** and has **Clear file list**. The list itself is not sent anywhere. When you ask the AI for a file by name it looks in this list and in your pinned folders first, and what it finds is handled like any other file the AI reads for you.
- **API keys:** stored encrypted with your operating system's keychain (Windows DPAPI, macOS Keychain, or the Linux secret service). If your system has no keychain, which happens on some Linux setups, Lumen doesn't save keys at all; set them as environment variables instead.
- **Background tasks:** the request, schedule, steps and result (which can hold text from pages the task read) are stored encrypted the same way, at most 50 tasks; with no keychain they aren't kept between sessions. A task sends data only to your AI provider and the sites you allowed for it. Deleting a task removes it.
- **Saved passwords (off by default):** with Settings → Privacy and security → **Save passwords** on, the logins you save are kept in one file in Lumen's profile folder, encrypted with your operating system's keychain the same way. Without a keychain the feature doesn't turn on; there is no unencrypted fallback. They never leave your computer: the AI in the sidebar, outside agents (MCP and CDP) and the page text Lumen sends with your messages can't read them, a password field's value is never included when the AI reads a page, and the AI can't run scripts on a site in a tab where you filled a saved password. Turning the feature off asks whether to delete them.
- **Meeting helper context and transcripts:** your meeting agenda, background notes, and transcript rolls are stored on your computer in your profile folder (`meeting-context.json` and saved chats). Audio capture and speech recognition run locally on your device; raw audio is never sent to remote servers. When you prompt the helper or request a summary, only the prompt text and selected meeting context are sent to your AI provider (or your local Claude Code CLI). Stealth mode uses operating-system window protection so call participants cannot see your meeting helper or notes during screen sharing.
- **Project chats as Markdown files:** a project can have a folder on your computer (by default `DocumentsLumen Projects<project>`), and the chats you keep in that project are also saved there as plain Markdown files, **not encrypted**: anyone who can open the folder can read them. Each project can turn this off, and chats outside projects are never written as plain files. Deleting a chat or a project never erases those files (they move to a `Deleted` or `Removed` folder inside the project's `Chats` folder). They stay on your computer.
- **AI memory (off by default):** with Settings → AI and agents → **Memory** on, the short notes the AI saves about you are kept in one file in Lumen's profile folder (`memory.json`), encrypted with your system's keychain the same way; with no keychain nothing is saved. Delete them one by one or all at once in Settings. They leave your computer only as part of the instructions sent to the AI provider of each chat (see below); temporary chats, background tasks and outside agents never get them.
- **Chats with the AI:** stored encrypted the same way, without the page text and tool results that went with them. If your system has no keychain, the chat isn't kept between sessions. **New chat** deletes the saved conversation.

## What websites are kept from learning

These protections are on by default and run on your computer. None of them sends anything anywhere. Pages can't tell when you leave their tab or window, or that your window is in split screen. Canvas, WebGL and audio fingerprints change from site to site. WebRTC offers only your public address. Tracking parameters are removed from the addresses you open, link-click pings and beacons are blocked, and Global Privacy Control is sent. Each one can be turned off in Settings → Privacy and security. What they cover and what they don't: [docs/privacy-protections.md](docs/privacy-protections.md).

## What leaves your computer, and to whom

- **What the AI reads in another app, if you let it.** With **Let the AI use other apps on this computer** on (off by default, Windows and macOS), the AI can read the windows of apps you allow one by one, and what it reads (names, text and values of controls, and a picture of the window when it asks) goes to the AI provider you picked as part of that chat, like a page does. Nothing goes anywhere else. Password fields are never read, and security prompts (Windows and macOS), password managers, System Settings, banking apps and Lumen's own window are never available. The live thumbnail on the card stays on your computer. On macOS, window pictures are taken with `screencapture` into a file in Lumen's temp folder that is deleted as soon as it has been read.

- **The AI you choose.** When you send a message, Lumen sends it to the provider you picked, with the page context it needs to answer: the text of the page you're on, and a screenshot when the AI asks for one. The provider can be Anthropic (directly or through Claude Code), OpenAI, xAI, Google or OpenRouter. Each provider's own privacy policy applies to what it receives. Nothing is sent to an AI until you ask it something or start a task. With a key saved, Lumen also asks that provider which models the key can use, to fill the model menu. With **Memory** on, your saved notes go with every chat's instructions to that provider, and with **Search past chats** on, short snippets of your earlier chats that the AI looked up go with that request.
- **What you say to voice input, if you use it.** When you record with the mic button in the chat box (or the Meeting helper's **Listen**), the recording goes once to the provider set in Settings → AI and agents → **Voice input**, with your own key: OpenAI (`api.openai.com/v1/audio/transcriptions`) or Google Gemini (`generativelanguage.googleapis.com`). Automatic uses OpenAI if you added an OpenAI key, otherwise Gemini. The audio goes nowhere else, is never written to disk, and is dropped from memory as soon as the text comes back; only the text stays, in the chat box. The microphone is on only while the button shows it recording. Lumen allows the microphone for its own sidebar and chat page only; websites still ask you as before. Set **Voice input** to Off to turn the button off.
- **The websites you visit,** as with any browser. The AI's background reading (`read_urls`) runs without your cookies, unless you let it use your signed-in account on a site: when it asks ("Let the AI use your signed-in example.com account?", off until you answer), you can say No, Just this once, or Always for that site. Allowed, it loads that page with your cookies in a background tab marked as the AI's, sees it as you do, and the page text goes to your AI provider like any page it reads. Nothing is clicked, typed or submitted there without the usual approvals. A redirect to any other site is read without your cookies. Banks, payment services, password managers and account-security pages can only be allowed once. The sites you allowed always are listed in Settings → AI and agents → Signed-in sites the AI can use, where you can remove them. Outside AI agents (MCP) and background tasks never read with your cookies. When the AI works in your own tabs (navigate, open_tab, clicking), it sees them as you do, signed in, as before.
- **Skills, to the AI you chat with.** A skill you run with `/` goes as your message. Skills you mark **AI can use this on its own** (and your Claude Code skills, when **Use my Claude Code skills** is on) are listed by name and one-line description in each new chat's system prompt, and the AI loads a skill's full text only when it calls `use_skill`. With **Offer to save and improve skills** on (off by default), a draft or a revision is written only after you click: the last exchange, the names of the tools used, the skill's text and your corrections go to the model in one call. Lumen reads Claude Code skills from `~/.claude/skills` on your computer and never sends them anywhere else.
- **Your search engine,** when you search from the address bar or the start page.
- **DuckDuckGo, for the AI's web search.** When the AI searches the web through Lumen's own search tool (OpenAI, Grok, Gemini and OpenRouter models, Claude Code and other MCP agents), Lumen loads DuckDuckGo's results page for the query without your cookies. Claude by API key or Anthropic sign-in uses Anthropic's own web search instead.
- **OpenAlex, Crossref, Semantic Scholar, arXiv and PubMed, for the AI's scholarly search.** When the AI uses `find_sources` (and `/research`), Lumen asks these public databases for papers matching the words the AI chose, and for the citations of one paper (a DOI). The requests carry no key, no cookies and nothing about you; the only thing that leaves is the search text. The Research board is stored with the chat (encrypted like the chat), and only sends anything out when you open a source in a tab.
- **A service you connect for the AI (Connectors).** Only after you press Connect in Settings → You and AI → Connectors: Lumen then sends requests to that service alone (Canvas at your school's address, GitHub, Todoist, Notion, Google Calendar's feed address, or Google for Gmail) when the AI uses it, to read or, after you confirm, add what you asked for. Outside agents over MCP can't use them. Canvas uses the sign-in you already have in Lumen; the others use a token you pasted, kept encrypted on this computer and never shown to the settings page again. What a service returns joins your chat, so it goes on to the AI you picked, like page text. Nothing goes to anyone else, nothing is cached on disk, and Disconnect deletes the token.
- **Component downloads.** Lumen downloads ad and tracker block lists (Ghostery's published lists) and Google's Widevine component, which lets DRM video play. Extensions you install come from the Chrome Web Store.
- **Telegram, if you connect your own bot (off by default).** With Settings → AI and agents → **Phone (Telegram)** on and a bot token saved, Lumen asks Telegram's Bot API (`api.telegram.org`) for new messages to your bot while it is open, and sends replies there: "Working on it…", the task's result, and its approval questions (the site and what the AI wants to do). So your requests, the results and those questions pass through Telegram's servers, and Telegram's privacy policy applies to them. The bot token is stored encrypted with the OS keychain and sent only to Telegram; the paired chat's id and name, the bot's username and the last message number are kept in `settings.json`. Remove bot forgets the token and the pairing.
- **Sign-in.** "Sign in with OpenRouter" opens OpenRouter's own sign-in page, and the key it returns is stored as described above.

### Smaller requests, and what triggers them

- **On-device translation language packs.** The first time you translate to or from a language on this device, Lumen asks, then downloads that language's pack (about 20 to 55 MB per direction) from Mozilla's servers: it reads Mozilla's public model list (`firefox.settings.services.mozilla.com`, cached for a week) and the files from `firefox-settings-attachments.cdn.mozilla.net`, and checks each file against the SHA-256 Mozilla publishes. The requests name the pack and carry nothing about the page you were on. The translation itself runs on your computer in a separate process; the page's text is never sent anywhere. Packs are kept in Lumen's data folder and can be deleted in Settings → General → Translation. Packs are not tied to a window: a pack downloaded from a private window stays in the data folder afterwards (it records which language you downloaded, never any page or site), so delete it in Settings if that matters to you. Translating with your AI or Google Translate is a different choice, covered by their own consent.
- **Site icons for the start page.** When a site that's in your favorites or most-visited list shows its icon, Lumen downloads a copy of that icon once (from the address the site names, often the site itself or its CDN) and keeps it on your computer, so the start page never has to load anything.
- **OpenRouter's model list.** With OpenRouter connected, Lumen downloads OpenRouter's public model catalog (`openrouter.ai/api/v1/models`) to fill the model menu and **More models…**. It's cached on your computer for a day.
- **The Anthropic CLI.** Clicking **Sign in with your Anthropic account** in Settings → AI and agents, when the `ant` CLI isn't already installed, downloads a pinned version from Anthropic's GitHub releases (`github.com/anthropics/anthropic-cli`) and checks it against a built-in SHA-256 checksum before installing it in Lumen's profile folder. Signing in then happens on Anthropic's own site.
- **Lumen updates.** Shortly after Lumen starts, every few hours, and when you click **Check for updates**, Lumen asks GitHub for the newest release of `emah-maker/lumen` and reads its `latest.yml` (`latest-mac.yml` on a Mac). The request carries nothing beyond what any download from GitHub does (your IP address and Lumen's user agent). An installed Windows copy with **Download updates automatically** on (Settings → About Lumen) then downloads the new installer from the same release. Development runs never check.
- **Extension updates.** If you've installed Chrome Web Store extensions, Lumen checks Google's update service (`update.googleapis.com`) for newer versions: at startup, when the window gets focus (at most once every 3 hours), and every 5 hours. The check sends the installed extensions' ids, your operating system and processor type, and the Chromium version. With no extensions installed, no check is made.
- **AI topic grouping.** If you turn on **Use AI to name and group topics**, Lumen sends the titles and site names (hostnames, not full addresses) of the tabs being grouped to the cheapest model of your chat's AI provider. This happens when you choose **Organize Tabs by Topic**, and, with **Group Automatically → By Topic** on, a few seconds after you have four or more ungrouped tabs. It's off by default.
- **Google Safe Browsing (off by default).** If you turn on **Warn about dangerous sites** (Settings → Privacy) and add your own Google API key, Lumen downloads Google's lists of suspected phishing and malware sites (`safebrowsing.googleapis.com`) and keeps them up to date, as often as Google allows. Each page you open is checked against these lists on your computer. Only when an address matches does Lumen send Google 4-byte partial hashes of it (never the address), without your cookies. Google works to provide the most accurate and up-to-date information about unsafe web resources. However, Google cannot guarantee that its information is comprehensive and error-free: some risky sites may not be identified, and some safe sites may be identified in error.

## AI agents over MCP

Settings → AI → **Let other apps use Lumen** gives Claude Desktop, Cursor, VS Code and Claude Code (outside Lumen) a token each. The tokens are kept encrypted with your system keychain in `external-apps.json` in the profile folder, and each also in a file of its own (`external-app-tokens`) that only your user can read, named by the text you paste into the app (which holds no secret); Revoke deletes both. Programs running as you can read those files, as with any local app; the list also keeps each app's name and when it was last used. Nothing is sent anywhere by this: the apps connect to Lumen on your computer only, they can't reach Lumen's settings, saved passwords or chat history, and what they read goes to their own provider under that app's policy. Lumen shows a pill while an app works and a quiet notification when it finishes. ([Details](docs/external-apps.md).)

If you turn on **Allow AI agents to connect**, AI apps on your computer (such as Claude Code, Codex, Antigravity or Cursor) can read and control your tabs through Lumen. They're asked before acting on a new site. This setting is off by default. Whatever those apps read is then sent to their own AI provider, under that app's privacy policy.

Settings → Usage can show your Codex plan limits and token totals. To do that Lumen reads the numbers (token counts, the used percentage and reset time of each limit window, the plan name) from the newest lines of Codex's own session logs in `~/.codex/sessions` on your computer, never your prompts, replies, file names or sign-in details, and keeps only those numbers in its `usage.json` like its other usage figures. "Add to Codex CLI" asks the `codex` program whether you are signed in and only adds one `[mcp_servers.lumen]` entry to `~/.codex/config.toml` (after copying the file); Lumen does not read `auth.json`.

When the sidebar AI still hands a task back to you after Lumen's one automatic second try, Lumen can note it in `diagnostics/ai-gaps.jsonl` in the profile folder: the date, the site's host name, a short category of what was missing, and the tool's error with addresses and quoted text removed. It holds no page content and none of your words or the AI's reply, it never leaves the computer unless you copy the list from Settings → AI → Show what the AI couldn't do and send it, and it can be cleared there or turned off (Keep a list of what the AI couldn't do).

Settings → AI also has optional **Give … full access to this computer** switches for Claude Code, Grok Build and Antigravity in the sidebar (all off by default, one each plus a master switch). With one on, that command-line AI can read any file on your computer and run commands, as it does in your terminal, and what it reads is sent to its own AI provider under that provider's privacy policy. Lumen does not see or keep it.

**Routine web links** (Settings → AI and agents → Routines, off by default) open a small listener on `127.0.0.1` (port 47821 unless you change it) so programs on your computer can start a routine. It accepts nothing from other computers, only requests addressed to `127.0.0.1` or `localhost`, and answers only `POST /routines/<id>/run` with that routine's own key, and keeps only a SHA-256 fingerprint of each key. Text sent with a request is stored with the routine's run (encrypted with the background tasks) and goes to your AI provider as part of that run. If you expose the port to the internet yourself (a tunnel for Zapier or IFTTT), anyone with the link and key can start the routine, at most once a minute, and its approvals still apply. A routine whose trigger is **When new email arrives** reads the unread list of your Gmail connector every few minutes, on your computer; only the new messages' sender, subject and first words go to your AI provider with that run.

The same goes for **Allow automation tools (Chrome DevTools Protocol)**, also off by default: while it's on, programs on your computer can read and control your tabs without asking.

## Removing your data

Settings → Privacy and security → Clear browsing data clears history, cookies and site data. Removing a key in Settings → AI and agents deletes it. To remove everything, delete Lumen's profile folder:

- Windows: `%APPDATA%\Lumen`
- macOS: `~/Library/Application Support/Lumen`
- Linux: `~/.config/Lumen`

## Music-reactive home screen and music cards

Off by default: Settings → Appearance → New tab page → "Music-reactive home screen", and "React to music" in a Spotify or Apple Music card's own settings. When either is on and the new-tab page is on screen, the page listens to the sound the PC is playing: Windows' system-output loopback, a copy of what is already going to the speakers, handed to the page by Lumen (audio only; the video track the browser insists on is stopped at once). It reads that with a Web Audio analyser to get a few loudness numbers (bass, mids, highs) about 30 times a second and moves a picture with them. The analyser is not connected to anything that makes a sound, and the copy never stops, mutes or delays any program. Lumen never uses the microphone, never records or stores audio, and nothing leaves the page or the computer: only those numbers are used, and only on the page. The listening stops the moment the page is hidden or both options are off. Windows only; elsewhere the option does nothing and says so.
